# Devices, financing, trade-in and upgrade offers: real T-Mobile vs judge sandbox

All customer data in this project is synthetic. The real-offer facts below are public information, and every source was accessed **2026-10-17**. The sandbox (`/workspace/tmobile/dubhacks/data/sandbox.db`, `metadata.as_of=2026-10-01`, brand "T-Mobile (stub data, not official)", schema v9) was opened **read-only** (`file:...?mode=ro`, SELECT only). Main rule: **customer texts may quote only $ amounts that exist in the sandbox `promotions`/`plans`/`addons` tables.** Real offers are for strategy, credibility and calibration only.

Main brief hook: **Direction #4 Timekeeper** (upgrade now vs wait, a transparent now-vs-later cost, a trade-in value chart, an honest "wait"). This material also supports #3 Next Best Moment (whether a lock-in/upgrade offer is a valid retention action) and #1 Cart Whisperer (trade-in and protection suggestions).

## 1. What changed in 2026 (context you need before reading the table)
- **Aug 6, 2026, "Nothing" launch:** new Experience More 2.0 / Experience Beyond 2.0 / Essentials 2.0 plans. Financing moved from 24 to **36 months** (EIP Standard 36 at 0% APR for everyone; EIP Flex 36 also finances taxes/fees/$35 DCC, with $0 upfront and 0% APR for well-qualified customers "for a limited time"). Sources: T-Mobile PR newsroom (en.t-mobilepr.com, Aug 4 2026) https://en.t-mobilepr.com/News/t-mobile-presenta:-nada-de-nada-paga-dollar0-hoy-por-un-telefono-nuevo-con-eip-flex ; https://www.t-mobile.com/switch/zero-out-of-pocket ; The Verge https://www.theverge.com/tech/975115/t-mobile-financing-eip-flex-36-plan
- **The yearly upgrade became "Early Upgrade".** You still need 50% paid + ≥6 months + a good-condition trade-in, but on a 36-month EIP that takes about **18 months** instead of 12. Experience More 2.0 **drops** the every-2-years upgrade perk. Sources: PhoneArena (Aug 6 2026) https://www.phonearena.com/news/t-mobile-nothing-plans_id182429 ; TmoNews (Aug 7 2026) https://www.tmonews.com/2026/08/t-mobiles-new-2-0-plans-are-live-but-your-upgrade-just-got-slower/ ; WhistleOut https://www.whistleout.com/CellPhones/News/ramageddon-is-coming-for-your-phone-plan
- ⚠️ **STALE/CONFLICTING:** the FAQ on t-mobile.com/cell-phone-plans still describes a "yearly upgrade" with "24 monthly bill credits", while the plan cards on the same page say "Early Upgrade". We treat the plan card and the 2.0 news coverage as current.
- **Bill-credit lock-in:** all current device promos are "up to 36 monthly bill credits". Terms: "Contact us before cancelling entire account to continue remaining bill credits, **or credits stop & balance on required finance agreement is due**" and "**Bill credits end if you pay off device early**". The promo FAQ confirms that customers enrolling on or after July 1, 2024 forfeit the remaining recurring device credits if they pay off early. Source: https://www.t-mobile.com/offers/promotional-offer-details . Credits also stop on a plan downgrade below the required plan, a JUMP! upgrade from a credited device, or returning one BOGO device: https://www.t-mobile.com/support/new-to-tmobile/promotional-cards-credits-and-rebates

**Finding: the sandbox's own `tradein_curves` (62 devices × ages 0–60) already fit the evidence well, so we use them and don't invent a curve.** They follow `value = 0.78 × MSRP × exp(−k × age_months)` with k by brand/tier: Apple flagship → 56% @12 mo, 40% @24, 28.5% @36, 20% @48 (matches SellCell iPhone 52–67% @12–13, 52% @25, 35% @37, 23% @49). Value tier → 51% / 33% / 21%. Foldable (Flip8) → 52% / 34% / 23%.

**Where the sandbox is generous:** Samsung/Google flagships at 12–24 months (sandbox about 54% / 38%; evidence about 40% / 29–31%), and the month-0 value (0.78 of MSRP; evidence says about 0.50–0.65 for Android, about 0.65–0.72 for iPhone).

### Proposed synthetic decay curve (for charts and devices missing from the sandbox; label "synthetic, calibrated to public resale data")
`v(age) = MSRP × r0 × exp(−k × age) × cond × sept_step`

| Segment | r0 | k (/mo) | → @12 | @24 | @36 | Calibrated against |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Apple flagship | 0.72 | 0.024 | 54% | 40% | 30% | SellCell 12-mo 51.5%; live table |
| Samsung/Google flagship | 0.58 | 0.030 | 41% | 28% | 20% | SellCell 39.5–40.8%; live S24/S23, Pixel 9/8 |
| Foldables | 0.55 | 0.037 | 35% | 23% | 14% | SellCell foldables 35.4% |
| Motorola / budget Android | 0.45 | 0.050 | 25% | 14% | 7% | SellCell Moto 24.5%; BankMyCell budget −42% Y1, −59% Y2 |

- `cond`: mint 1.00, good 0.92, poor 0.78, faulty 0.05 (Android) / 0.18 (iPhone) of the good value (SellCell).
- `sept_step` (optional, for iPhones only): ×0.85 in the month after a new iPhone launches (Decluttr −15% first month), then flat. A simpler option is to skip it and note "values dip after new-model launches".
- **Recommendation for the engine:** use sandbox `tradein_curves` as-is (judge data) for all numbers. Show the parametric curve only as a "public-data sanity band" on the Timekeeper chart, or use it for the "what if you sell it yourself" comparison.

## 4. How this drives Timekeeper / EV logic (proposed rules, all synthetic)
1. **Credits remaining = switching cost.** For a line with an active 36-month credit promo: `forfeit_if_leave = value_cents × months_left/payout_months` + the remaining EIP balance due. A customer with a large forfeit is low flight risk mechanically, so a discount there wastes money (supports "do nothing can win").
2. **Upgrade now vs wait** (per line): `cost_now = remaining_EIP − promo_credit_new(eligible) − tradein_value(age)` vs `cost_wait_m = same at age+m` (with the trade-in value decayed by the curve and the remaining EIP reduced by m installments). Say **"wait"** when the device is <24 months old, still financed, with no new-line/trade-in promo eligible, and the trade-in drop over the next 6 months is smaller than the installments saved.
3. **Flat "any condition" promos beat the market for old or damaged phones.** If `tradein_curve(age) < $200`, `PROMO-STUB-TRADEIN-ANY` is strictly better than selling. For an iPhone under 18 months old, the market value can exceed carrier credits, so be honest: "you'd get more selling it yourself."
4. **Early Upgrade is a retention re-lock (Beyond only):** eligible when `months_since_purchase ≥ 6` and `1 − balance/MSRP ≥ 0.5`. It re-locks for 36 months but costs the carrier the remaining 50%. Count that in offer cost.
5. **Protection:** suggest the P360 tier that matches `devices.protection_tier` only for financed, high-MSRP devices with no protection add-on. Never for BYOD old phones where the monthly price × 12 > the trade-in value.

## 5. Sandbox gaps / data-quality notes (for tm-offers / tm-strategy)
- ⚠️ **`lines.tradein_eligible` = 1 on 6,575 financed lines.** Real T-Mobile requires the trade-in device to be **paid off** (support/devices/promo-trade-in), except through Early Upgrade/JUMP!, where the carrier pays it off. The engine should require `financing_balance_cents = 0` OR an Early-Upgrade path before counting trade-in value.
- No trade-in **tiers** ($1,000/$700/$500 by trade-in model) in the sandbox: iPhone promos are flat.
- No BOGO, Early Upgrade, JUMP! or EIP Flex promo rows. `PROMO-STUB-OLD-PHONE-UPGRADE`, `LOYALTY-CREDIT` and `CARE-CREDIT` have `source='stub'` (no public equivalent).
- Sandbox P360 deductibles ≠ real 2026 deductibles (monthly prices do match).
- Device age buckets (as of 2026-10-01, non-BYOD lines with a date): 0–11 mo 5,603 · 12–23 2,543 · 24–35 1,127 · 36–47 454 · 48+ 346. 2,171 lines are ≥50% through a 24-month EIP. 800 paid-off, trade-in-eligible lines are ≥36 months old.

## 6. Uncertainty flags
- The 2026 plan/promo facts come from t-mobile.com plus 2026 tech press. T-Mobile's own FAQ text is internally inconsistent (yearly vs early upgrade).
- BOGO status relies on r/tmobile community posts (unofficial). T-Mobile's page shows no live BOGO.
- tmo.centers.diy is an unofficial promo mirror, used only to corroborate t-mobile.com wording.
- Depreciation numbers are resale-market (SellCell/BankMyCell), not T-Mobile's non-promo trade-in quotes, which are typically lower. BankMyCell and Decluttr data are 2019–2022.
